How to Weigh an RV: Complete Guide

Weighing your RV is one of the most important safety checks you can perform. This guide walks you through the entire process, from preparation to interpreting your results.

⚠️ Why This Matters

An overloaded RV or improperly distributed weight can lead to:

  • Tire blowouts and brake failure
  • Poor handling and increased stopping distance
  • Suspension damage and frame stress
  • Voided manufacturer warranties
  • Legal liability in case of an accident

Before You Weigh

1. Know Your RV's Weight Ratings

Find these numbers on your RV's VIN sticker (usually on the driver's side near the door):

  • GVWR (Gross Vehicle Weight Rating): Maximum total weight your RV can safely carry
  • GAWR (Gross Axle Weight Rating): Maximum weight each axle can carry
  • UVW (Unloaded Vehicle Weight): Weight of the RV as it left the factory
  • CCC (Cargo Carrying Capacity): How much cargo you can add

2. Prepare Your RV

Weigh your RV as you typically travel:

  • Full fresh water tank (or at your typical travel level)
  • Full propane tanks
  • All passengers who normally travel with you
  • All cargo, including food, clothing, tools, and equipment
  • Fuel tank at typical traveling level (at least half full)
  • All accessories (bikes, chairs, grills, etc.)

💡 Pro Tip

Many RVers are surprised to find they're significantly overweight once they load everything they want to bring. It's better to find out at a scale than on the highway!

Types of Weighing

Option 1: Single Platform Weigh (Basic)

Drive your entire RV onto a truck scale. This gives you your total weight but doesn't show weight distribution between axles.

Cost: Usually $10-15 for one weigh

Good for: Quick check to ensure you're under GVWR

Option 2: Individual Axle Weighing (Recommended)

Weigh each axle or set of wheels separately. This is the gold standard for RV weighing because it reveals weight distribution problems.

Process:

  1. Drive onto scale so only front axle is on the platform
  2. Record front axle weight
  3. Drive forward so only rear axle(s) are on platform
  4. Record rear axle weight(s)
  5. For towables, weigh the tow vehicle and trailer separately

Cost: Some locations charge per weigh (typically $2-3 per additional weigh after the first)

Option 3: Four-Corner Weighing (Most Accurate)

Weigh each wheel position individually using portable scales or specialized equipment. This reveals side-to-side weight distribution.

Where: Some RV rallies and service centers offer this, or you can purchase portable wheel scales

Cost: $20-50 for professional service; $200-600 for portable scales to own

Step-by-Step: Weighing at a CAT Scale

CAT Scales are the most common option for RVers because they're widely available at truck stops.

1 Find a CAT Scale: Use the WeighHere directory or visit catscale.com

2 Call Ahead: Verify the scale is operational and get instructions for RVs

3 Enter the Scale: Follow posted signs. Usually you'll drive across the scale completely, then stop when your entire vehicle is on the platform

4 Stay in Vehicle: Keep the engine running and everyone inside while they weigh you

5 Get Your Ticket: Go inside to pay ($14 for first weigh) and get your weight ticket

6 Weigh Individual Axles: Ask to do additional weighs for each axle. Most CAT scales allow this for $2.50 per reweigh

Interpreting Your Results

Check Total Weight

Your total weight must be less than your GVWR. Ideally, stay at least 10% below the maximum.

⚠️ If you're over GVWR: You must remove cargo. There's no way around this - it's unsafe and illegal to exceed your GVWR.

Check Axle Weights

Each axle weight must be less than its GAWR. Front axles are often the limiting factor in Class A and Class C motorhomes.

Check Tire Capacity

Divide the axle weight by the number of tires on that axle. Each tire must be rated for at least that weight at your inflation pressure.

Example: If your rear axle weighs 10,000 lbs and has 4 tires, each tire is carrying 2,500 lbs. Check your tire sidewall to ensure each tire is rated for at least 2,500 lbs at your current PSI.

Check Weight Distribution

For tandem axles (two axles close together), weight should be relatively even. If one axle is carrying significantly more than the other, you may have a suspension problem or need to redistribute cargo.

What If You're Overweight?

If your weigh reveals you're over limits:

  1. Remove cargo: Start with heavy items and things you don't really need
  2. Redistribute weight: Move items forward or backward to balance axle loads
  3. Empty tanks: Travel with less water (you can refill at campgrounds)
  4. Upgrade tires: If you're under GVWR/GAWR but over tire capacity, higher-rated tires may help
  5. Consider upgrades: Some chassis can be upgraded with heavier springs or axles, but consult a professional

⚠️ Never Exceed GVWR or GAWR

Tire upgrades can help with tire capacity, but they don't increase your GVWR or GAWR. The frame, brakes, and suspension are still limited to the manufacturer's ratings.

How Often Should You Weigh?

  • Initial weigh: Before your first major trip with a new RV
  • After modifications: Any time you add significant accessories (solar, generator, etc.)
  • Seasonally: At least once per year, or if you've changed your typical cargo
  • Before long trips: If you're carrying more than usual
