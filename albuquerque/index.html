Public scales in Albuquerque, New Mexico (I-40 and I-25)

Three CAT Scales verified on the operators’ own location pages in Albuquerque: Flying J #689 on the west side (I-40 Exit 153), TA Albuquerque #0081 at the Big I (I-40 Exit 159A/D, I-25 Exit 225/227) and Pilot #1106 on Broadway at the south edge of town (I-25 Exit 215). Useful for U-Haul and PPM moves through New Mexico, RVs and fifth-wheels on I-40 or I-25, and horse and stock trailers heading for the Rio Grande Valley. West on I-40, see Gallup / Jamestown / Milan / I-40; east, see Moriarty / Santa Rosa / I-40.

This page lists 3 places to weigh near Albuquerque, NM: 3 CAT or truck-stop scales. All are in Albuquerque. 3 are listed as likely to print a certified weight ticket, the kind PPM movers and RV owners checking weight ratings usually need. CAT platforms have a 2,000 lb minimum and do not give corner weights.

CAT Scales in Albuquerque

We list only stops verified on the operator’s own location page. Flying J #689 and Pilot #1106 include CAT Scale in their store offerings and say Open 24 Hours. TA Albuquerque lists CAT Scale among its amenities and says fuel is available 24/7, but publishes no 24-hour store statement, so we leave its scale hours blank. None of the three publishes a CAT-staffing schedule. 2,000 lb floor. No corner weights. Weigh the whole rig, then drop the trailer and weigh the tow vehicle for the trailer number. Do not unload horses at a truck stop. New Mexico weighmaster rules differ from California — confirm ticket needs with your TO or licensing office. Use CAT’s locator for other stops.

Do not go here for a ticket

New Mexico ports of entry on I-40 and I-25 are for commercial vehicles — not a place to buy a civilian weighmaster ticket for a U-Haul, RV, boat, horse trailer, or PPM load. Follow posted signs; do not treat this directory as a bypass guide.

Towing gear that helps before you weigh

As an Amazon Associate, WeighHere earns from qualifying purchases. Links open an Amazon search, not a specific product we tested.

  • Tongue weight scale — Check hitch weight in your driveway before the CAT run; a common target is roughly 10–15% of trailer weight on the ball.
  • Weight distribution hitch — Spreads heavy tongue weight across both axles of the tow vehicle so the front end stays planted on travel and horse trailers.
  • Trailer brake controller — Lets you set and trigger electric trailer brakes from the cab — worth having before a loaded trailer meets a long downgrade.
  • RV / trailer tire pressure monitor (TPMS) — Watches trailer tire pressure and heat while you drive; an underinflated tire on a heavy load is a common blowout.
  • Wheel chocks — Hold the trailer still while you unhitch, level, or measure tongue weight.
  • Moving hand truck / dolly — For PPM / DITY movers: load boxes and appliances between your empty and full weigh tickets without a second helper.
