Car weight scales near me: how to weigh your car or pickup truck

Want to know what your car or pickup really weighs, or how much payload you have left before a move or a tow? Here is what the labels on your door already tell you for free, which scales near you will take a passenger vehicle, what a weigh costs, and how to do it, from federal vehicle rules, CAT Scale, and scale operators’ own pages.

This is a plain-language summary of official pages, read on 25 Nov 2026. Prices change, so call the scale before you drive. WeighHere is independent and not affiliated with CAT Scale, CDFA, the DMV, NHTSA, or any scale operator.

The short answer

  • Free first step: read the two labels by the driver’s door. One gives your GVWR (the most the loaded vehicle may weigh) and axle ratings; the other says how much people plus cargo you may carry. That tells you the limits. Only a scale tells you where you are against them.
  • Cheapest real weigh: a dedicated public scale house. They set their own prices; two in San Diego County publish $10 and $25.
  • Walk-up, no appointment: a CAT Scale at a truck stop, $15.25 for a full weigh and $5.25 for a reweigh within 24 hours. CAT’s floor is 2,000 lb.
  • Not here: highway weigh stations are for enforcement and do not sell you a weight. Landfills and recycling yards weigh their own customers’ loads and may refuse a walk-up.

Sources: CAT Scale FAQ, Q12, Q13, Q22 (prices checked 25 Nov 2026) · Allstate Logistics public scales · Eckert’s public scale

What the labels on your door already tell you

You do not need a scale to find your vehicle’s limits. Federal rules put them on the vehicle:

  • The certification label (49 CFR 567.4) is fixed to the hinge pillar, the door-latch post, or the door edge next to the driver’s seat. It lists the Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR) in pounds and a Gross Axle Weight Rating (GAWR) for each axle, front to rear. The rule says GVWR can’t be less than the unloaded vehicle weight plus its rated cargo load plus 150 lb for each seating position.
  • The tire and loading placard (49 CFR 571.110) sits on the driver’s side B-pillar. It reads “The combined weight of occupants and cargo should never exceed XXX kilograms or XXX pounds,” along with seating capacity and the recommended cold tire pressures.
  • Curb weight is the weight of the vehicle with standard equipment and a full load of fuel, oil, and coolant (the definition in 49 CFR 571.110). Look for it in your owner’s manual or the manufacturer’s spec sheet.

The owner’s manual has to show you how to use the placard (49 CFR 575.6): take the occupants-and-cargo number, subtract the weight of the driver and passengers, and what is left is your cargo and luggage capacity. The rule’s own example: with a 1,400 lb figure and five 150 lb passengers, 650 lb is left for cargo. If you tow, some of the trailer’s weight moves onto your vehicle and cuts that number.

The labels can’t tell you what your car or truck weighs today: with a toolbox, a camper shell, a roof box, a lift kit, a full bed, or the family on board. That is what a scale is for.

Sources: 49 CFR 567.4 (certification label) · 49 CFR 571.110 (tire placard, curb weight) · 49 CFR 575.6 (load-limit steps)

Is there a free truck scale near me?

Probably not one you can count on. We could not find an official list of free certified scales, and every price we could verify for a walk-up weigh was a paid ticket. California’s Division of Measurement Standards describes its Public Scales Listing as privately owned facilities that offer truck-weighing services; each sets its own fee. (The listing was offline for maintenance when we checked on 25 Nov 2026; try again later.)

Places with scales that are not free ticket shops:

  • Highway weigh stations enforce commercial-vehicle rules. You can’t walk in and buy a weight for your car. See public scale vs highway weigh station.
  • Landfills, transfer stations, quarries, and scrap yards weigh loads so they can charge for material. Many won’t weigh a walk-up who is not a customer. See dump trailer & landfill scales.

So the cheapest honest route is: read the labels for free, then pay for one weigh at the lowest-cost certified scale near you.

Sources: CDFA Public Scales Listing · CDFA Weighmaster Enforcement Program

Weighing a car or pickup at a CAT Scale

CAT says its scales are set to a 2,000 lb threshold. If your vehicle or combination is estimated to be lighter, it asks you to call the 24-hour Help Desk at 1-877-CAT-SCALE (228-7225), ext. 6, before you pull on. More on that in CAT’s 2,000 lb minimum.

CAT’s scale has three platforms. For a truck and trailer, it says to put the steer axle (front wheels) on platform 1 and the drive or rear axle on platform 2, with the trailer on platform 3. The whole vehicle must be on the scale. You get axle weights and a gross weight. CAT does not weigh individual wheels or corners and does not allow that kind of weighing.

No appointment is needed. Pull on, press the intercom button on the sign by platform 1, give the cashier your details, then pull off and go inside for the ticket. Check it before you leave: CAT says tickets are legal documents and can’t be changed after they print.

Sources: CAT Scale, How To Weigh · CAT Scale FAQ, Q1, Q20, Q22

How to weigh your car or pickup, step by step

  1. Decide what you need. Just a number for your own peace of mind? Any certified scale will do. A signed certificate for the DMV, a military move, or a buyer? Ask them what must be printed first (see weight certificates and PPM weight tickets).
  2. Load it the way you drive it. Passengers, tools, gear, and the trailer if you tow. To compare with the published curb weight, weigh empty with a full tank, since curb weight includes full fuel.
  3. Weigh. At a public scale house, follow the attendant. At a CAT Scale, front axle on platform 1, rear axle on platform 2.
  4. Do the math. GVWR minus the gross on your ticket is the payload you have left. Compare each axle weight with its GAWR on the door label. If you tow, weigh hooked up and unhooked to find tongue weight; see how to check tongue weight.
  5. Keep the ticket. CAT keeps an electronic record of its tickets for seven years and takes copy requests online.

Sources: 49 CFR 567.4 · 49 CFR 571.110 · CAT Scale FAQ, Q8

Pickup owners in California: weight and registration

California DMV says pickup trucks can be registered as passenger or commercial vehicles depending on how they are used. For commercial registration, DMV defines a pickup as a commercially used motor truck with a manufacturer’s GVWR under 11,500 lb, an unladen weight under 8,001 lb, and an open box-type bed under nine feet long. Pickups are left out of the REG 4008 declaration that other commercial vehicles of 6,001 to 10,000 lb unladen must file. DMV may ask for a weighmaster certificate for a commercial vehicle with an unladen weight of 10,000 lb or less. Our weight certificate guide covers when, the exceptions, and what must be on it.

Sources: California DMV, Commercial Vehicle Registration · DMV 13.145 Weight Certificate

Frequently asked questions

Where can I weigh my car near me?

Two walk-up options take most cars and pickups: a dedicated public scale house (the kind on California's CDFA Public Scales Listing) or a CAT Scale at a truck stop. Highway weigh stations are enforcement sites, not places to buy a weight. Landfills, quarries, and recycling yards have scales too, but they weigh material for their own customers and may turn away a walk-up.

Is there a free truck scale near me?

We could not find an official list of free certified scales, and every scale price we could verify is a paid ticket. The free part of the job is reading your vehicle's labels: the certification label by the driver's door gives GVWR and axle ratings, and the tire placard on the driver's side B-pillar says how much occupants plus cargo may weigh.

How much does it cost to weigh a car?

It depends on the scale. CAT Scale lists $15.25 for a full-price weigh and $5.25 for a reweigh within 24 hours at the same scale; in California a Weighmaster Certificate is $30.50 and $10.50. Public scale houses set their own fees: Allstate Logistics lists $25 for a certified weight and Eckert's in San Marcos lists $10 for an empty and heavy weight on one ticket (both checked 25 Nov 2026). Call first.

Can a CAT Scale weigh a car?

CAT says its scales weigh vehicles between 2,000 lb and 200,000 lb and are set to a 2,000 lb threshold. If your vehicle or combination is estimated to be lighter than that, CAT asks you to call its 24-hour Help Desk at 1-877-228-7225, ext. 6, before you weigh. CAT gives axle and gross weights, not individual wheel (corner) weights.

Where do I find my car's weight without a scale?

Federal rules require a certification label next to the driver's seat (hinge pillar, door-latch post, or door edge) showing the Gross Vehicle Weight Rating and Gross Axle Weight Rating for each axle, and a tire and loading placard on the driver's side B-pillar with the maximum combined weight of occupants and cargo. Curb weight, the empty weight with full fuel, oil and coolant, is usually in the owner's manual or the manufacturer's specs.

How do I work out my pickup's payload?

Weigh the truck loaded the way you drive it, then compare the scale's gross weight with the GVWR on the door label and each axle weight with its GAWR. For people and cargo, federal owner's-manual rules give the method: take the placard's occupants-and-cargo figure and subtract the weight of the driver and passengers; what is left is what you can carry. Tongue weight from a trailer also counts against it.
