Guide · Travel trailer · Tongue weight

How to weigh a travel trailer and check tongue weight at a CAT Scale

A truck-stop scale can tell you what your loaded travel trailer, horse trailer, boat or utility trailer really weighs, and how much of it is sitting on your hitch. It takes two passes over the scale and a little subtraction. Here is the method, the numbers to compare against, and where to find a scale.

This guide uses CAT Scale’s own How To Weigh page, the Oregon DOT / NHTSA brochure Towing a Trailer: Being Equipped for Safety, and Nissan’s 2026 Towing Guide, read on 29 Oct 2026. Your own vehicle and trailer manuals always win. WeighHere is independent and not affiliated with CAT Scale Company.

Why the sticker weight is not enough

Trailer brochures usually quote a dry weight. Oregon DOT’s towing brochure says that figure is not what matters: to pick a tow vehicle and hitch, you need to know how much the trailer weighs fully loaded, including water, propane, gear, a boat or motorcycle, and anything else you carry. The only way to know that number is to weigh the trailer packed the way you travel.

Tongue weight matters just as much. Oregon DOT calls it the amount of the trailer’s weight that presses down on the hitch. Too little can make the trailer sway. Too much can take weight off the tow vehicle’s front wheels so it steers less well.

The ratings you are checking against

Find these on the tow vehicle’s door-jamb label, in its owner’s manual, and on the trailer’s VIN label. Oregon DOT defines them this way:

RatingWhat it limits
GVWR (gross vehicle weight rating)What the tow vehicle may weigh fully loaded, including the tongue weight it carries
GCWR (gross combination weight rating)The tow vehicle, trailer, passengers, equipment and fuel together
GAWR (gross axle weight rating)What a single axle can carry. Oregon DOT says the rating on the certification label must not be exceeded
Towing capacity and max tongue weightListed in the owner’s manual and can vary with equipment

For a conventional ball-hitch trailer, Nissan’s 2026 towing guide says to keep tongue load between 10% and 15% of the actual trailer weight, and never above the vehicle’s maximum tongue load. It adds that some trailers need different numbers, so follow the trailer maker. Fifth-wheels and goosenecks use different ranges; check your manual.

Step 1: weigh the whole rig

Load everything as you would for a trip, with full tanks if you travel that way. CAT’s instructions for a truck and trailer are to get the whole combination on the scale with the steer axle on platform 1 and the drive or rear axle on platform 2. The trailer will normally show up on platform 3. For a truck and boat, and for a truck and fifth-wheel, CAT gives the same positions.

Press the intercom button, tell the weighmaster it is a first weigh, and say you will be back for a reweigh. Then pull off and pay inside. Our CAT Scale cost guide has current prices, including the cheaper reweigh.

From this ticket you get:

  • Gross combined weight: the total of all three platforms. Compare it with the tow vehicle’s GCWR.
  • Tow vehicle axles, hitched: platforms 1 and 2. Compare each with its GAWR, and their sum with the tow vehicle’s GVWR.
  • Trailer axles: platform 3. Compare it with the trailer’s axle ratings.

If you use a weight-distributing hitch, do this pass with the bars set the way you tow. Oregon DOT notes that the hitch shifts tongue weight among all the axles, which is exactly what you want to check here.

Step 2: weigh the tow vehicle alone

Unhook the trailer somewhere you are allowed to leave it. Do not unhook on the scale. Ask the fuel desk where you may drop it. Then drive the tow vehicle back on with the steer axle on platform 1 and the rear axle on platform 2, and ask for a reweigh.

Keep the same people and cargo in the vehicle for both passes, or the subtraction will be off.

Step 3: do the math

You wantCalculation
Tongue weight (what the hitch carries)Tow vehicle hitched (platforms 1 + 2, first ticket) minus tow vehicle alone (reweigh)
Loaded trailer weightGross combined weight minus tow vehicle alone
Tongue weight as a share of trailer weightTongue weight ÷ loaded trailer weight × 100

Nissan’s guide gives its own example: for a 1,500 lb trailer, 10% is 150 lb. Run the same multiplication on your loaded trailer weight to get your target range, then compare your measured tongue weight. If you are low, Nissan says to move some load forward. If you are high, move load back or lighten the trailer, and stay under your vehicle’s maximum tongue load either way.

With a weight-distributing hitch engaged, the first ticket shows how load is spread, not the full tongue weight. If you need the true tongue number, do an extra pass with the bars released before you unhook.

What a CAT Scale will not do

  • Wheel or corner weights. CAT says its scales give axle and gross weights but cannot weigh each corner, and it does not allow that kind of weighing. For side-to-side balance you need a separate wheel-position weighing service. See our RV and fifth-wheel page.
  • Very light loads. CAT scales are set to a 2,000 lb threshold. An empty utility trailer by itself may not read. See CAT’s 2,000 lb minimum.
  • Ticket edits. CAT says a printed ticket is a legal document and cannot be altered afterward.

Hauling animals? Read weighing a horse trailer before you pull into a busy truck stop. And a highway weigh station is not where you go for this. See public scale vs highway weigh station.

Frequently asked questions

Can a CAT Scale weigh my trailer tongue directly?

No. A CAT Scale reads platform weights, not the load on your hitch ball. You find tongue weight by comparing two weighs of the tow vehicle: once hitched and once alone. The difference on the tow vehicle’s axles is the weight the trailer was putting on it.

What should tongue weight be on a travel trailer?

Follow your tow vehicle and trailer manuals first. Nissan’s 2026 towing guide, for example, says to keep conventional tongue load between 10% and 15% of the actual trailer weight and never above the vehicle’s maximum tongue load. Fifth-wheel and gooseneck hitches use different ranges, so check your own manual.

Is the trailer’s dry weight good enough?

No. Oregon DOT’s towing brochure notes that manufacturers often publish a dry or empty weight, but to choose a tow vehicle and hitch you need to know what the trailer weighs fully loaded. Weigh it packed the way you travel.

Do I have to unhook the trailer on the scale?

No, and you should not. Weigh the combination, pull off, drop the trailer somewhere you are allowed to park, and come back for a reweigh of the tow vehicle alone. Ask the fuel desk where you may unhook.

Does a weight-distributing hitch change the numbers?

Yes. Oregon DOT explains that a weight-distributing hitch spreads tongue weight across the tow vehicle and trailer axles. The bars move load between axles, so a weigh with bars engaged shows how your axles are loaded, not the full tongue weight. Weigh once with the bars engaged to check axle ratings.

What if my trailer and truck are under 2,000 lb?

CAT says its scales are set to a 2,000 lb threshold, so a small utility trailer on its own may not register. Weigh it hitched, or use a dedicated public scale house. Call CAT’s help desk at 1-877-228-7225, ext. 6, if you are unsure.
